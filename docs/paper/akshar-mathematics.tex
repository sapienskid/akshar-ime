\title{The Mathematics of AksharIME:\\ A Source-Channel Transliteration Engine\\
\large Classical foundations, empirical corrections, and the information budget\\ of romanized Devanagari}
\author{AksharIME Project}

\begin{abstract}
We give a complete mathematical treatment of AksharIME, a dependency-free
transliteration engine for romanized Devanagari that attains $80.69\%$
top-1 accuracy on the Aksharantar Nepali benchmark \citep{aksharantar},
exceeding the published neural state of the art, IndicXlit
\citep{indicxlit}, at $80.25\%$. The engine is classical: a joint
source-channel model \citep{li2004} trained by expectation--maximization,
a Kneser--Ney syllable language model \citep{kneserney1995}, decoded by
beam search over a syllabic lattice \citep{koehn2004}, and a corpus-frequency
prior over the candidate list. We present the full derivations, four
corrections to the classical treatment that proved essential (output-identity
preservation in beam merging, joint backoff targets, weighted observation
EM, and floating-point poisoning guards), an empirical decomposition of the
\emph{information budget} of romanized input, and the negative result that
grammars trained on alignment posteriors cannot be additively fused with
deterministic language models. We outline the parameter-free context-tree
formulation \citep{ctw1995} that this analysis motivates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Romanized input --- typing an Indic language on a Latin keyboard --- is an
\emph{information-lossy} encoding problem: the roman string $\R$ under-determines
the target Devanagari word $D$ (vowel length, schwa, and nasalization are
not encoded). Neural sequence-to-sequence models \citep{indicxlit} dominate
published benchmarks but require frameworks, millions of parameters, and
per-language retraining. This work documents a deliberately classical
engine --- expectation--maximization \citep{dempster1977}, Kneser--Ney
smoothing \citep{kneserney1995}, and weighted finite-state-style decoding
\citep{hellsten2017} --- that matches the neural state of the art while
remaining a few megabytes of counted statistics, and states precisely where
its accuracy ceiling lies: in the entropy of the roman encoding, not in the
model.

Our contributions beyond the classical treatment of \citet{li2004}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an output-identity-preserving beam merging rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:merge}),
\item the joint backoff target for pair grammars over latent alignments (\S\ref{sec:joint}),
\item floating-point poisoning guards for forward--backward over learned tables (\S\ref{sec:fp}),
\item weighted-observation EM with an equivalence to replication (Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted}),
\item the corpus-frequency prior as an interpolated MAP layer (\S\ref{sec:vocab}),
\item backward self-training via emission argmax (\S\ref{sec:selftrain}),
\item an empirical information budget of romanized Devanagari (\S\ref{sec:budget}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem formulation}

Let $\R = r_1\cdots r_m$ be the roman input and $D = a_1\cdots a_n$ a
Devanagari word, a sequence of \emph{aksharas} (orthographic syllables).
An \emph{alignment} $\sigma$ assigns each akshara $a_j$ a contiguous chunk
$s_j$ of $\R$ of length $l_j \in [1, L]$, $L = 5$, or the empty chunk;
$\Sg(\R, D)$ denotes the set of valid alignments. The engine computes
\[
\argmax_D \; P(D \mid \R) \;=\; \argmax_D \sum_{\sigma \in \Sg(\R,D)}
P(\R \mid D, \sigma)\, P(D) \;=\; \argmax_D P(\R \mid D)\, P(D),
\]
the last step by independence of $\sigma$ given $D$ (total probability over
the alignment space). Inference is shortest-path search over a weighted
lattice in the tropical semiring $(\mathbb{R}^+ \cup \{\infty\}, \min, +)$;
all probabilities are stored as $-\log$ weights.

\section{The V1 engine: classical source-channel mathematics}

\subsection{Emission model and EM training}

Following \citet{li2004}, the emission model factorizes over the alignment:
\begin{equation}
P(\R \mid D) \;=\; \sum_{\sigma \in \Sg(\R,D)} \prod_{j=1}^{n} P(s_j \mid a_j),
\label{eq:emission}
\end{equation}
where each $P(s \mid a)$ is a multinomial over roman chunks per akshara.
Since $\sigma$ is unobserved, parameters are estimated by EM
\citep{dempster1977}. Define the forward and backward recurrences over the
alignment lattice (1-based akshara index, $L = 5$):
\begin{align}
f[0][0] &= 1, \qquad
f[j][i] = \sum_{l=1}^{\min(L,i)} f[j{-}1][i{-}l]\; P\big(r_{i-l+1..i} \,\big|\, a_j\big),\\
b[n][m] &= 1, \qquad
b[j][i] = \sum_{l=1}^{\min(L,m-i)} P\big(r_{i+1..i+l} \,\big|\, a_j\big)\; b[j{+}1][i{+}l],
\end{align}
with word likelihood $Z = f[n][m]$ and the posterior of a specific alignment
edge
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{post}(j, l, i) \;=\; \frac{f[j{-}1][i{-}l]\; P(r_{i-l+1..i} \mid a_j)\; b[j][i]}{Z}
\;\le\; 1 .
\label{eq:post}
\end{equation}
The M-step with Dirichlet smoothing toward the global chunk unigram is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{new}}(s \mid a) = \frac{c(a,s) + \alpha\, P_{\mathrm{uni}}(s)}
{\sum_{s'} c(a,s') + \alpha}, \qquad \alpha = 0.05 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Kneser--Ney syllable language model}

$P(D)$ is factorized by a trigram Kneser--Ney LM \citep{kneserney1995} over
the akshara sequence, counted \emph{deterministically} from gold
segmentations (not from EM posteriors; cf.\ \S\ref{sec:dilution}):
\begin{align}
P_{\mathrm{KN}}(b \mid a) &= \frac{\max\big(c(a,b) - \delta,\, 0\big)}{c(a)}
\;+\; \lambda(a)\, P_{\mathrm{cont}}(b),\\
\lambda(a) &= \frac{\delta \cdot |\{b : c(a,b) > 0\}|}{c(a)}, \qquad
P_{\mathrm{cont}}(b) = \frac{|\{a : c(a,b) > 0\}| + \tfrac12}{|\Sg_{\mathrm{bigram}}| + \tfrac{N}{2}},
\end{align}
with absolute discount $\delta = 0.75$ \citep{kneserney1995}, plus a
word-initial prior $P(a \mid \#) = (c_\#(a) + \tfrac12)/(W + \tfrac{N}{2})$.
The continuation count measures the number of \emph{distinct left contexts}
of $b$; it is the component that penalizes akshara sequences never observed
in any context, which raw-count smoothing under-penalizes.

\subsection{Decoding}

The lattice DAG has nodes (roman position, LM context) and edges
(chunk length, akshara) weighted $w(e) = -\log P(s \mid a)$. Exact inference
is Viterbi dynamic programming in the tropical semiring
$(\mathbb{R}^+ \cup \{\infty\}, \min, +)$; the engine uses beam search with
width $B$ and the k-best list feeds a re-ranking stage.

\section{Corrections to the classical treatment}

\subsection{Output identity in beam merging}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:merge}
Beam states $(pos, \text{LM context})$ may be merged only if the spelled
output strings coincide. For transliteration lattices, distinct paths
sharing a context can spell distinct strings: the roman chunk \texttt{na}
aligns to both aksharas \nda{} (na) and \naa{} (n\=a).
\end{proposition}
Merging by context alone replaces the cheaper spelling with the other and
the deleted candidate can never be regenerated --- measured at $-12$ points
of top-1 in the v2 decoder. The fix is a path-hash in the deduplication key
(a splitmix64 avalanche of the akshara path), preserving output identity.

\subsection{Joint backoff targets for pair grammars}\label{sec:joint}
A pair grammar $P(\pi_j \mid \pi_{j-1})$ over aligned pairs
$\pi_j = (a_j, s_j)$ must back off to the \emph{joint} unigram
$P(a,s) = P(a)\,P(s \mid a)$, not to $P(s \mid a)$ alone: the latter assigns
a rare akshara with a single observed chunk $P(s\mid a) \approx 1$, making
junk paths with rare aksharas nearly free. Measured: $6.5\% \to 18.4\%$
top-1 from this correction alone.

\subsection{Floating-point poisoning of forward--backward}\label{sec:fp}
For words the model assigns near-zero probability, $Z$ reaches $10^{-14}$
or below. Although every posterior in \eqref{eq:post} is bounded by 1 in
exact arithmetic, in floating point a single degenerate word contributed
$\sim\!10^{14}$ units of false posterior mass, destroying the trained
statistics (accuracy collapsed from $18.4\%$ to $6.5\%$). The guards
\[
\text{skip if } Z < 10^{-12} \text{ or } Z \notin \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{finite}};
\qquad \mathrm{post} := \min(\mathrm{post}, 1)
\]
restore the invariant. We state this as a required condition for any
forward--backward trainer over learned tables: \emph{the posterior bound
\eqref{eq:post} is an exact-arithmetic statement; floating-point
implementations must enforce it.}

\subsection{Weighted observation EM}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:weighted}
Let a training pair carry an integer observation weight $w$. Then EM with
all count contributions and posteriors scaled by $w$ yields the same
estimates as replicating the pair $w$ times.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Both the corpus likelihood objective and every count in the E- and M-steps
are linear in the multiplicity of a pair: replication multiplies the
forward--backward statistics by $w$, and the M-step normalizes per pair, so
the parameter update is identical. Scaling posteriors by $w$ directly
achieves the same without $w$-fold storage.
\end{proof}
This lets deduplicated synthetic training files carry frequency as an
explicit field; equivalence was verified empirically (identical $80.69\%$).

\subsection{The corpus-frequency prior as interpolated MAP}
The k-best list from the decoder is re-scored by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}'(v) = \mathrm{score}(v) - \lambda \ln\!\big(1 + f(v)\big),
\label{eq:vocab}
\end{equation}
where $f(v)$ is the frequency of candidate $v$ in running text. This is a
log-linear mixture: the decoder contributes $\log P(v \mid \R)$ and
\eqref{eq:vocab} contributes a prior interpolated between uniform and the
corpus empirical distribution (exact at $\lambda = \alpha$ in the
$\alpha$-interpolated sense). Two quantitative findings:
(i) the prior's value is bounded by candidate depth --- it re-ranks only
what the decoder emitted, so the SOTA configuration required
$B{=}256$, $k{=}50$ jointly ($80.46\%$, versus $78.84\%$ at $k{=}8$);
(ii) the prior is informative only if the corpus frequency distribution is
itself informative: mined word-pair corpora have near-uniform native-side
frequencies (2.4M unique words over 2.4M tokens) and contribute nothing,
while 75M tokens of running text (570k distinct words, Zipf-distributed)
contribute $+3.5$ points.

\subsection{Backward self-training via emission argmax}
The inverse map $D \to \R$ is deterministic under the model: the canonical
romanization of $W$ is $\arg\max_s P(s \mid a)$ per akshara, and runner-up
chunks are spelling variants \emph{actually produced by users} in the
corpus. Because variants are whole-akshara token substitutions, the
segmentation alignment is invariant by construction; variants are weighted
by joint probability and $\tfrac14$ decay. This manufactures training pairs
whose word distribution matches real usage (fixing the alignment bias of
\S\ref{sec:dilution} at the data level), worth $+0.23$ top-1.

\section{Empirical results}

On the Aksharantar Nepali test split \citep{aksharantar} (4{,}101 pairs;
native-word subset $n{=}2108$), strict top-1:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\hline
System & Native top-1 \\
\hline
IndicXlit (11M-parameter transformer) \citep{indicxlit} & 80.25\% \\
V1 baseline (beam 64, no vocabulary prior) & 75.33\% \\
V1 + vocabulary prior (k = 8) & 78.84\% \\
\textbf{V1 + vocabulary prior ($B{=}256$, $k{=}50$, $\lambda_{lm}{=}0.85$)} & \textbf{80.46\%} \\
\quad + backward self-training pairs & \textbf{80.69\%} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Named-entity subsets improve likewise (AK-NEI $39.2\% \to 44.8\%$).
Decoding latency is $10.8$\,ms/word at beam 256 ($3$\,ms at beam 64);
incremental prefix decoding (work in progress) targets the IME keystroke
budget.

\section{The information budget of romanized Devanagari}

Define the ambiguity set $\A(\R) = \{D : P(\R \mid D) > \tau\}$. The oracle
curves (gold present in the top-$k$ of the decoder) are the empirical CDF
of the gold rank:
\[
P(\text{gold} \in \text{top-}k):\; 75.3\ (k{=}1),\ 85.2\ (2),\ 88.0\ (3),\ 89.7\ (5),\ 92.1\ (50).
\]
The gap $75.3 \to 92.1$ is \emph{ranking error}; the remaining $7.9$ points
are \emph{coverage error}. Critically, $52\%$ of top-1 misses differ from
the gold only in vowel-length or nasal signs (\emph{matra-only} errors):
both candidates belong to $\A(\R)$ with comparable priors, so
\emph{no function of $\R$ alone} can separate them. Each external
information source shrinks the effective ambiguity set:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\hline
Evidence source & Acts on & Measured \\
\hline
grammar + LM (string only) & ranking within $\A(\R)$ & 75.33\% \\
word frequency prior & $\A(\R) \cap$ Words, weighted & 80.46\% \\
word-bigram context & $P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})$ & pending (E6) \\
user history (on-device) & personal $P(w)$ & compounding \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The plateau at $\approx 80.7\%$ is thus an empirical measurement of the
conditional entropy of romanized Devanagari under this grammar: it is a
property of the encoding, not of the model.

\section{A negative result: alignment-bias in posterior-trained grammars}

Our v2 grammar conditioned transitions on the previous aligned pair
$P(\pi_j \mid \pi_{j-1})$, trained on EM posteriors. It underperformed the
classical baseline by $12$ points at every fusion weight. The reason is
structural: posterior mass concentrates on the \emph{most probable
alignment per corpus pair}, which is frequency-biased toward misalignments
($P(\text{\texttt{na}} \mid \naa) \approx 0.97$ versus
$P(\text{\texttt{na}} \mid \na) \approx 0.35$ makes word-initial
\texttt{nam} mass onto \naa{}+\mmaa{} even for words whose gold aksharas
are \na{}+\mma). The pair table therefore encodes \emph{alignment
frequency}, not word structure, while the deterministic akshara LM encodes
word structure. Additive fusion of the two injects the bias at any positive
weight (measured: all $\lambda_{\mathrm{pair}} > 0$ reduced top-1). The
resolution is not a better fusion weight but a single estimator that treats
context depth as latent --- the context-tree weighting formulation of
\citet{ctw1995}, which selects the context length per event by Bayesian
evidence and carries a regret bound of $|T|\ln 2$ nats against the best
fixed-depth model in hindsight. This is the v3 design and the subject of
ongoing work.

\section{Conclusion}

A classical source-channel engine, with four targeted corrections to the
standard treatment and a corpus-frequency MAP layer, exceeds the published
neural state of the art for romanized Devanagari at $1/10$th the artifact
size and zero runtime dependencies. The information-budget analysis explains
both the ceiling of string-only transliteration and the headroom that
contextual evidence provides. All code, training data sources, and
measurements are open.
